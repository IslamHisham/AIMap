From clipboards to AI

How businesses get mapped has barely changed. Here's the route, and where AIMap takes it.

  1. Stop 1

    On foot

    Cities hire students every summer to walk the streets and log businesses by hand. A whole summer covers a small area.

  2. Stop 2

    By car

    Drive-by surveys cover more ground, but every business is still recorded manually.

  3. Stop 3

    Directories and web maps

    Instant, but incomplete. Google Maps lists fewer than half of the businesses we find.

  4. Now

    AIMap

    AI fuses every source, checks that each business is operating and keeps the map current. Hours, not months.

Hard to build, harder to copy

A continuously updated pipeline turns messy, scattered data into one validated map of every business.

What goes in

OpenStreetMap, Google and Apple Maps, government data, directories, Yellow Pages, Facebook and the open web.

What happens

ML entity resolution merges duplicates, language models sort everything into one taxonomy, and every business gets a confidence and provenance score. Incremental updates catch openings and closures.

What comes out

Live today: a validated, always-current map of every business, with a question-and-answer prototype. Next: site ranking with explanations.

Coverage no other map has

We find more than twice the businesses Google Maps lists in our coverage area, and every one is checked as operating.

Entity resolution at scale

The same café shows up under different names, addresses and categories across sources. Our models merge them into one scored record.

History no one can backfill

We log every opening, closure and move. A competitor starting today can't recreate that timeline.
